# Startup IQ Assessment Specification

**Rubric version:** v1.0  
**Assessment date:** 16 October 2026 (foundation baseline)

## Dimensions and weights

| Dimension | Weight | Focus |
|---|---:|---|
| Market Potential | 20% | Customers, market assumptions, urgency, reachability, evidence |
| Problem & Solution Clarity | 15% | Specific problem, affected users, fit, differentiation, validation |
| Competitive Position | 15% | Direct/indirect alternatives, switching costs, advantage evidence |
| Business Model | 15% | Customer, value proposition, pricing, revenue, acquisition, delivery |
| Financial Readiness | 15% | Revenue, costs, pricing logic, cash, unit economics, consistency |
| Risk Management | 10% | Market, operational, technical, legal, security, execution risks and mitigations |
| Pitch Readiness | 10% | Narrative, communication, evidence, traction, financial story, completeness |

Total weight: **100%**.

## Deterministic scoring

Each dimension rating is 0–100. `Overall Score = SUM(Dimension Rating × Dimension Weight / 100)`.

Reference case: 70, 80, 60, 75, 65, 70, 90 produces contributions 14, 12, 9, 11.25, 9.75, 7, and 9, for an expected **72.00 / 100**.

### Provisional interpretation

0–20: not yet defined or unsupported; 21–40: major gaps; 41–60: developing with limited validation; 61–80: promising structure with meaningful gaps; 81–100: strongly supported against the rubric, subject to verification. These are guidelines, not scientifically validated predictors of success.

## Evidence policy

Record supported information, estimates, unvalidated hypotheses, and unknown information separately. Missing information is not automatically a zero. Provisional ratings must explain uncertainty and missing evidence. AI may summarize and explain, but the deterministic scoring engine alone applies the final weighted score.

## Planned intake fields

Basic information: startup name, industry, stage, short description. Problem and solution: problem, affected people, solution, value proposition, evidence. Market: customers, geography, market assumptions, research, demand evidence. Competition: direct and indirect alternatives, differentiation, supporting evidence. Business model: revenue, pricing, acquisition, delivery. Financial assumptions: costs, revenue, cash, pricing, unit economics. Traction and team: progress, interviews/trials, traction, capabilities, gaps. Risks: business, technical, operational risks and mitigations.

Evidence records will contain a title/description, related dimension, source name or URL, date, status, and notes. Claims must be clearly distinguished from verified evidence. Founders may mark information as unknown; idea-stage startups are not required to have traction.

## Rule-based recommendations

The recommendation engine uses the deterministic assessment result, rating thresholds, evidence status, and missing-evidence flags. Ratings below 60 receive high-priority improvement guidance. Ratings from 60 through 79 receive refinement guidance. Ratings of 80 or above do not produce an unnecessary warning when supporting information is available, but weak evidence still produces a validation action. A low rating combined with weak evidence produces one combined recommendation rather than contradictory messages.

Recommendations identify the related dimension, priority, issue, explanation, recommended action, suggested evidence or validation method, and reason. They ask the founder to obtain missing information and never invent customer research, market statistics, sales, financial results, or external sources. These are transparent rule-based prompts, not scientifically validated predictions or AI-generated conclusions.
